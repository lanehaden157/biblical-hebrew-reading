## L3.1 The story tense
unlocks: M:w_seq

Biblical stories move forward with one verb form: va- plus a doubled letter.
{Gen.1.3#1} vayyómer "and he said", {Gen.1.4#1} vayyar "and he saw", {Gen.1.5#1} vayyiqra
"and he called".

Read it as "and then he did X". English uses a plain past tense, and the "and" often goes
untranslated.

A run of these verbs is the backbone of a story: each is the next event. When a clause breaks
the run (a noun first, or another verb form), it usually gives background or starts a scene.

The doubling is the clue: vayy-, vatt-, vann-. Ve- plus a verb without doubling is a different
form (Unit 4).

## L3.2 Who: the prefix letter
unlocks: M:y3ms, M:t3fs, M:a1cs, M:n1cp

After va- comes a letter that says who acted:
- y- he: {Gen.1.3#1} vayyómer "and he said"
- t- she: {Gen.3.2#1} vattómer "and she said". The same t- can mean "you".
- ' (alef) I: {Gen.24.39#1} va'omar "and I said". Alef cannot double, so va'- has no
  doubling.
- n- we: {Gen.42.31#1} vannómer "and we said"

In narrative, he and she do almost all the work. I and we appear when a character retells
events.

## L3.3 They: a final -u
unlocks: M:y_u, M:t_u

A final -u makes the verb plural: {Gen.11.3#1} vayyomeru "and they said".

y- with -u is "they"; t- with -u is "you (pl.)": {1Sam.12.12#9} vattómeru "and you said".

So check three things on a story verb: va- with doubling (story tense), the prefix letter
(who), a final -u (more than one).

The subject noun usually follows the verb and matches it: {Gen.1.12#1-2} vattotse ha'árets
"and the earth brought forth". The t- agrees with the feminine noun "earth".

## L3.4 Short forms
unlocks: M:short

Many common verbs end in -eh in their ordinary form: {Gen.22.14#7} yir'eh "he will see",
{Gen.18.25#19} ya`aseh "he will do". The story tense drops the ending: {Gen.1.4#1} vayyar "and
he saw", {Gen.1.7#1} vayyá`as "and he made".

Others shorten a vowel: {Jonah.3.9#3} yashuv "he will turn", but {Gen.22.19#1} vayyáshov "and
he returned".

vayehi (L1.9) is the short story form of yihyeh "he will be".

Doubling and prefix letter are unchanged, so the story tense is still easy to spot.

## L3.5 Finding the root
unlocks: F:7200:HC/Vqw3ms:e8aa7a

Under each verb is a root, usually three consonants, which is what a dictionary lists.

To find it in a story verb, strip va-, the prefix letter and any final -u. What is left is the
root: {Gen.20.2#8} vayyishlach is sh-l-ch "send", {Gen.14.14#1} vayyishma` is sh-m-` "hear",
{Gen.36.32#1} vayyimlokh is m-l-kh "reign".

If only two consonants are left, one has dropped out: {Gen.1.4#1} vayyar is r-'-h "see", whose
final h dropped. {Gen.22.19#1} vayyáshov is sh-v "return", whose middle letter is gone.

Unit 7 explains which letters drop and why. Until then, the whole forms you are learning cover
the common cases.

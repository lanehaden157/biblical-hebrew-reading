# Unit 2. Nouns and their attachments

Lesson source; syntax as in unit0.md. `unlocks:` lists the item ids whose first appearance
shows this lesson.

## L2.1 Plurals: -im and -ot
unlocks: M:im, M:ot

Most masculine nouns make the plural with -im: {Gen.18.16#3} ha'anashim "the men",
{1Kgs.5.6#6} susim "horses".

Most feminine nouns take -ot: {Gen.5.26#15} uvanot "and daughters".

The pairing is a tendency, not a rule: {Gen.4.19#5} nashim "wives" is feminine with -im. The
ending tells you "plural"; the gender belongs to the word.

A few words are plural in form but singular in sense: elohim "God", panim "face",
{Gen.1.6#6} hamáyim "the water", {Gen.1.8#4} shamáyim "sky".

## L2.2 Feminine -ah and the pair ending -ayim
unlocks: M:ah_f, M:ayim

A stressed final -ah usually marks a feminine singular noun or adjective: {Gen.5.10#12} shanah
"year", {Gen.12.19#8} le'ishah "for a wife".

-ayim marks a pair, or "two of": {Exod.21.21#5} yomáyim "two days", {Gen.34.21#13} yadáyim
"hands". Body parts that come in pairs use it: hands, eyes, feet, ears.

An unstressed -ah is a different ending, "toward" (L2.8). The transliteration's stress mark
tells them apart.

## L2.3 "Of": noun chains
unlocks: M:ey, M:at

Hebrew says "the word of YHWH" by setting two nouns side by side: {Gen.15.4#2-3} devar- YHWH.
The first noun is in its "of" form (construct); a maqqef often joins the two.

The first noun never takes ha-. The whole chain is definite when the last word is a name or
has "the".

Cues on the first noun:
- shorter vowels: davar "word" becomes devar.
- -im becomes -e: {Gen.42.5#2-3} bene yisra'el "the sons of Israel".
- -ah becomes -at: {1Kgs.10.4#2} malkat- "the queen of".

{Exod.13.18#5-6} dérekh hamidbar "the way of the wilderness".

## L2.4 Adjectives come after the noun
unlocks: L:1419_a

An adjective follows its noun and matches it in number, gender and "the":
{Gen.1.16#8-9} hama'or hagadol "the great light", literally "the light the great".

{Gen.1.16#4-6} shene hame'orot hagedolim "the two great lights": plural noun, plural
adjective.

When the noun has "the" and the adjective does not, or the adjective comes first, it is
usually a statement with no verb: "the X [is] great". Compare {Gen.1.4#5-6} ki- tov "that [it
was] good" (L1.8).

## L2.5 Pronouns: I, you, he, they
unlocks: L:859_a

Standalone pronouns: {Gen.6.17#1} va'ani "and I" (also anokhi), {Gen.7.1#5} atah "you",
hu "he", {Gen.3.12#7} hi "she", {Gen.42.11#7} anáchnu "we", atem "you (pl.)", hem "they".

A pronoun often makes a sentence with no verb: {Gen.15.7#3-4} ani YHWH "I [am] YHWH".

A story verb already shows who acts, so a pronoun beside a verb adds stress: "I myself".

hu, hi and hem after a noun with "the" mean "that, those": {1Sam.1.3#2-3} ha'ish hahu "that
man".

## L2.6 This and these
unlocks: L:428

zeh "this" (masculine), zot "this" (feminine), éleh "these".

After a noun with "the" they take "the" too: {Gen.7.13#2-3} hayom hazeh "this day",
{1Kgs.3.19#3-4} ha'ishah hazot "this woman".

With no "the", they start a sentence: {Gen.46.8#1-2} ve'éleh shemot "and these [are] the
names of".

## L2.7 Endings for my, your, his
unlocks: M:s_3ms, M:s_3fs, M:s_3mp, M:s_3fp, M:s_2ms, M:s_2fs, M:s_2mp, M:s_1cs, M:s_1cp

"His", "my" and "their" are endings on the noun: {Gen.12.17#8} beto "his house",
{Gen.3.20#4} ishto "his wife".

The same endings on a preposition give "to him", "with me": {Gen.3.9#6-7} vayómer lo "and he
said to him", {Gen.3.12#9} li "to me", {Gen.3.11#4} lekha "to you", {Gen.13.1#10} `imo "with
him". On 'et they make the object: {Gen.5.2#5} otam "them".

The core set: -i my/me, -kha your, -o his/him, -ah her, -nu our/us, -khem your (pl.),
-am or -hem their/them.

A noun with an ending is already definite ("his house"), so it never takes ha-.

## L2.8 After a plural: -av, -ay, -ehem
unlocks: M:p_3ms, M:p_3fs, M:p_3mp, M:p_3fp, M:p_2ms, M:p_2mp, M:p_1cs, M:p_1cp

On plural nouns the same endings come after a yod: {Gen.50.13#3} vanav "his sons",
{Gen.42.37#8} vanay "my sons", {Josh.5.7#2} benehem "their children".

So -o is "his" with one thing and -av "his" with several: {Gen.9.24#9} beno "his son",
vanav "his sons". Pairs count as plural: {Gen.27.16#7} yadav "his hands".

Some prepositions take this set too: 'el "to", `al "on", 'achare "after", tachat "under":
{Gen.8.9#8} elav "to him".

## L2.9 Toward: unstressed -ah
unlocks: M:dir

An unstressed -ah on a place word means "toward": {Gen.12.10#6} mitsráymah "to Egypt",
{Gen.18.6#3} ha'óhelah "into the tent".

The stress mark sits before it, which tells it apart from the feminine -ah (L2.2), which is
stressed.
